Some people have an easier time dealing with stress than others, and some bounce back more quickly. We call this resilience, and researchers are eager to find out how it works so that we can help people be more resilient. Unsurprisingly, some of that research has centered on serotonin. I should emphasize however, that everything I am discussing here is very much up in the air. I've seen more confusing and straight-up contradictory results while reading for this topic than any other. Accordingly, I will be presenting a proposed theory for serotoninergic resilience, and then I will highlight what seems at odds with it.

In the 90s the Deakin/Graeff hypothesis was proposed. The researchers suggested two different roles for different serotonin-releasing brain regions, the dorsal and medial raphe nuclei (DRN and MRN). The DRN, they suggest, suppresses innate fear and panic while promoting learned fear. The MRN in contrast mediates resilience to stress.

I have criticism of this interperetation of the DRN, but as the topic of this post is resilience, we will save that for another time and focus on the MRN. The MRN doubtless does many other things besides promoting resilience, as much research shows. Most research argues that it is the neurons that signal from the MRN to the hippocampus which are important for resilience.

The hippocampus is known to be important for memory formation. Recall from serotonin-and-learning that changes in the strength of the connections (called synaptic plasticity) between neurons are believed to be the main way memory is encoded in the brain.

The way this is proposed to work is this: during prolonged stress, the MRN releases serotonin into the hippocampus. This serotonin activates 5HT1A receptors in the hippocampus which block the formation of memories by inhibiting synaptic plasticity.

first contradiction: some studies show 5HT1A enhances plasticity in hippocampus, which was also proposed as a mechanism of resilience

This proved to be the hardest blog post to write. Initially I was interested in analyzing a specific hypothesis about the roles of two serotoninergic brain regions. Articles about this hypothesis generally focus on experiments that are consistent with it, but I wanted to read more broadly and find experiments that challenge it as well. In so doing, I found myself running into so many experiments whose results are straight-up contradictory. I can see no way to reconcile them, and would need replications in order to trust them.
