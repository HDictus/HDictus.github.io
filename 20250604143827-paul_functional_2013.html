(The first few paragraphs do provide a nice summary of some of the preliminary evidence given for the hypothesis.)

Paul and Lowry examine projection patterns from the raphe nuclei and argue that they are consistent with the Deakin/Graeff hypothesis.

On the basis of these data, they refine and further specify the hypothesis. Rather than the DRN as a whole giving rise to both kinds of effects, they propose that DRNd and DRNc promote learned anxiety, while the DRNvl inhibits innate fear. alongside the MRN, they propse that also the DRNi promotes resilience to chronic stress.

(One point that rubs me the wrong way about this hypothesis is that some people with panic disorder display freezing behavior as a part of their panic attack. The idea that panic disorder is then a dysfunction of fight/flight suppression doesn't make sense in these cases. It could be that at the time, these freezing events were not considered panic attacks and so were not in this disorder)

Paul and lowry argue that serotoninergic neurons in the dorsal and caudal part of the DRN inhibit fight/flight, the ventrolateral part potentiate learned anxiety, and the interfasicular part of DRN as well as MRN promote resilience.

They base this on the projection targets of these regions, to the extent they were known at that time.

interfascifulcar-dorsal-raphe-nucleus and median-raphe-nucleus both innervate the septohippocampal system. Theta rythms are proposed to have an important role in anxiety and fear-based learning, while several antidepressants disrupt theta rythm. The MRN suppresses hippocampal theta rythms. (huang-median-2022 suggests that this is not from serotoninergic neurons)

They note that there is some evidence of multiple subpopulations in both the MRN and DRN that respond differently to aversion and reward.

Takeaways:

  • the different parts of the DRN are distinct in terms of gene expression, while one group forms both MRN and DRNvl, others form other parts. This may be related to their projection patterns.
  • apparently alcohol relapse is used in animal models as a measure of stress? I didn't know animals could rehab in the first place.
  • lesioning median-raphe-nucleus leads to alcohol relapse, increasee stress is inferred to be the mechanism.
  • lesioning median-raphe-nucleus in the elevated-T-maze increases inhibitory (conditioned) avoidance.
  • inhibitory avoidance in the elevated-T-maze increases activity in dorsal-dorsal-raphe-nucleus, caudal-dorsal-raphe-nucleus, interfascifulcar-dorsal-raphe-nucleus and median-raphe-nucleus, but not ventrolateral-dorsal-raphe-nucleus.
