  • Takeaways

Kawai et al. demonstrate that DRN and MRN neurons process reward in opposite ways.

They cite nagai-role-2020, liu-dorsal-2014 for the claim that DRN stimulation is rewarding. (Interestingly, I had always heard it wasn't. I think I did encounter one of these studies before - it is a specific subset of neurons that they stimulated. see also li-serotonin-2016, zhong-learning-2017) Since systemic administration of SSRIs is not rewarding, they propose that the MRN acts in opposition to the DRN.

Other studies have investigated whether the MRN is rewarding or punishing, but they did not specifically target 5HT neurons.

Kawai et al. recorded activity from serotoninergic populations in the MRN. They found that when the mouse licked sugar water, these neurons reduced their activity. This was sensitive to the concentration of sugar. This was not wholly explainable by the termination of locomotion from the mouse.

When the tails of the mice were pinched, in contrast, these neurons increased their firing. This was not explainable by subsequent locomotion. Additionally, in thirsty mice, activity was decreased by licking water, but less so if that water contained quinine. An auditory cue associated with foot shock also activated MRN 5HT neurons.

Optogeneically inhibiting serotoninergic neurons in the median-raphe-nucleus was reinforcing. Mice were more likely to spend time in a room where they recieved optogenetic inhibition. Additionally, brief inhibition when the mouse poked a button with their nose led to steady increase of nose poking.

Optogenetically activating serotoninergic neurons in the median-raphe-nucleus was aversive. Mice spent less time in a room where the MRN was optogenetically stimulated. Mice also avoided drinking water that was paired with MRN stimulation when other water was available. Their licking of sugar water was also reduced by pairing it with stimulation.

To better understand how these neurons influence behavior they looked at the connectivity of the MRN. The median-raphe-nucleus strongly innervates the interpenduncular-nucleus, which is implicated in nicotine preference. It also innervates medial-habenula and hippocampus. In the IPN but not HBm or HP they found signs of increased neural activity in the group that recieved stimulation. In HP the overall change was nonsignificant, but if they looked only at non-pyramidal cells there was a significant increase in activity.

They inhibited serotoninergic axons in IPN, HPv, and HBm and saw that inhibition in IPN was strongly, and HPv weakly, rewarding. Conversely, stimulating these axons had the opposite effect: stimulation was aversive.

They found that when they administered WAY-100635 and MDL-100907, antagonists to 5HT1ARs and 5HT2ARs respectively, the aversive effects of MRN stimulation were blocked.

Not mentioned in their results, but rather in the discussion, they saw a slight increase in activity before licking. This may mean that the MRN also encodes reward anticipation.

  • they found that median-raphe-nucleus innervated interpenduncular-nucleus, medial-habenula, and hippocampus
