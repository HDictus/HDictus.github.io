quick-animal-well-review

Animal well is not strictly speaking a metroidbrainia, but the way it introduces its tools and unlocks makes it play a lot like one. In a metroidbrainia like Outer Wilds, you progress by understanding better how the world works. In the case of Animal well, you have to find a variety of tools to progress, like in a standard metroidvania. But the usage of those tools turns out to be a bit more intricate than you expected - each one has multiple uses and ways it interacts with the various elements of the environment. Perhaps part of what makes this work is that there are a lot of environmental elements to interact wihth. There were a number of moments where you get that feeling of genius that a good puzzle game or metroidbrainia gives you, which is usually absent from more straightforward metroidvanias. Figuring out those "advanced" mechanics was satisfying, and I wonder how the game facilitates your doing so, id like to replicate it.

I also really appreciated the uniqueness of the tools - at least the ones I unlocked, I'd never seen anywhere before. I'd have liked if the game revealed a little more of the mystery that unifies its themes: why are the tools all children's toys, why all the animals? I didn't go far into the postgame though. From what I looked up though, morsels of background or story are hard to come by there too. I feel like it is a shame in a game about discovery, about curiosity, not to have more of a plot or background to uncover. Though I guess leaving things ambiguous promotes its own sense of mystery.

The art was really beatifully done. In particular I think a lot of the shaders were very good, and I liked the effect they used to have the ghosts stream around you in the lantern. Occaisionally, some of the decor looked like spikes or similar, and caused confusion.

The last fight was a bit of an anticlimax - you had to use your tools in isolation, but quicky with the lantern. A genuinely difficult puzzle requiring you to combine all your tools would have been preferable - the boss was too easy to keepaway with the lantern. A multi-phase fight where you had to use each tool to overcome some dangerous aspect of the boss would be good too. The simple fact is the manticore was much easier than the dog or the chameleon, and so facing it was a bit of an anticlimax.

I do reccomend it, however.
